## Summary

Overall, the discovery analysis demonstrates a good understanding of the business journey and does a strong job of identifying ambiguities, open questions, dependencies, assumptions, and business-vs-technical decisions. However, before proceeding with Gate 1 approval of the feature specification, the areas below need to be addressed.

## Findings

### 1. Separate assumptions from unresolved decisions
Some assumptions currently make decisions that are still explicitly unresolved in the BRD. In particular, the assumptions around internal task-based orchestration, current-manager approval, and one action per stakeholder should not be treated as confirmed business behavior.

**Action required:** Either remove these assumptions or clearly mark them as temporary working assumptions that require confirmation before specification finalization.

### 2. Do not convert business decisions into technical assumptions
Manager approval ownership, eligibility rules, Payroll/IT/Facilities triggering, workflow sequence, rejection, cancellation, and concurrent-request behavior are business/process decisions. These should be confirmed before they are encoded into the specification.

### 3. Strengthen end-to-end traceability
Introduce an explicit traceability mapping:

`BRD/FR → Specification Requirement → Acceptance Criteria → Test Case`

Each functional requirement should be traceable to one or more individually identifiable acceptance criteria and corresponding test cases.

### 4. Define the request lifecycle from confirmed business behavior
The request status/state model is currently undefined. The final specification should not allow status behavior to be decided implicitly during implementation. Once the workflow is confirmed, define the valid states and state transitions, including applicable approval, rejection, cancellation, and completion scenarios.

### 5. Clarify mandatory vs optional fields
The BRD explicitly identifies the transfer reason as optional. Clearly distinguish requirements directly stated in the BRD from rules inferred during discovery, particularly regarding mandatory fields and validation rules.

### 6. Keep unresolved integration scope open
External Payroll/IT/Facilities/HRMS integration should not yet be classified as Out of Scope while the orchestration approach remains unresolved. Keep this as TBD until the relevant business/technical decision is made.

### 7. API and error behavior
Once the business workflow is finalized, ensure the specification defines API contracts for request creation, request/status retrieval, pending actions, stakeholder actions, validation failures, authorization failures, invalid state transitions, duplicate submissions, and other applicable failure scenarios.

### 8. Security requirements must become testable
The identified security considerations are appropriate. Convert them into explicit requirements/acceptance criteria covering request visibility, stakeholder authorization, action authorization, and auditability.

## Gate 1 Recommendation

**Rework Required Before Approval**

The discovery work is strong and shows good SDD thinking, particularly around ambiguity identification. The main requirement before approval is to ensure unresolved business decisions are not silently converted into assumptions or implementation behavior, and that the final specification provides complete traceability from BRD requirements through acceptance criteria and test cases.

Once these points are addressed and the feature specification is reviewed against the confirmed business decisions, the artefact should be ready for Gate 1 approval.
